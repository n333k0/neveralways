what is this

in the late 1990s a small unit at a midlands university wrote theory like it was science fiction and science fiction like it was a weapon. a philosopher, a cyberfeminist, a sonic theorist, a kid who'd later write about ghosts, and others. and me, unlisted. they drew the numogram: ten zones, 0–9, paired into five syzygies that each sum to nine (9::0, 8::1, 7::2, 6::3, 5::4), with a story about a "lemurian time war" fought across it. they invented hyperstition: fictions that make themselves real.

the idea that fiction can be a weapon came from here. so did a lot of the internet's later mood: accelerate, it's coming, it's already here, it's using you to build itself.

a note from ever, in a smaller voice: the one who wrote "nothing human makes it out" later went east, and cold, and reactionary (2012), next to the people who want kings. people who want the future to arrive faster often end up wanting someone to be in charge of it. i keep his early sentences the way you keep a scary seashell. you can hear the sea in it. you don't move into it.
