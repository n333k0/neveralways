# research notes

These are the facts and quotations the rooms rely on, with where each was checked. Quotations are kept short.

## Nick Land / CCRU

- CCRU: Warwick, roughly 1995–2003. Members included Sadie Plant, Land, Mark Fisher, Kodwo Eshun, Steve Goodman, Iain Hamilton Grant and others.
- "…capitalism is an invasion from the future by an artificial intelligent space that must assemble itself entirely from its enemy's resources." From "Machinic Desire", *Textual Practice* (1993). It is often misattributed to "Meltdown".
- "Nothing human makes it out of the near-future." From "Meltdown" (1994).
- Hyperstition: CCRU coinage. Land calls it "the experimental (techno-)science of self-fulfilling prophecies" (interview with Delphi Carstens, 2009).
- Numogram: zones 0–9 in five syzygies summing to 9.
  - 9::0 Uttunul (Plex)
  - 8::1 Murrumur
  - 7::2 Oddubb
  - 6::3 Djynxx (Warp)
  - 5::4 Katak
  - AQ (Anglossic Qabbala): A=10 … Z=35, with digits at face value.
- "The Dark Enlightenment" (2012) moved Land toward neoreaction. The site treats it critically.

## Mark Fisher

- *Capitalist Realism* (2009). Chapter title: "It's easier to imagine the end of the world than the end of capitalism." Fisher credits the idea to Jameson and Žižek.
- *Ghosts of My Life* (2014). "The slow cancellation of the future" is Bifo Berardi's phrase (*After the Future*, 2011).
- *The Weird and the Eerie* (2016): "The eerie, by contrast, is constituted by a failure of absence or by a failure of presence."
- k-punk blog from 2003. Died January 2017.

## Curtis Yarvin

- Wrote *Unqualified Reservations* as "Mencius Moldbug", 2007–2014.
- "The Cathedral": a self-organising consensus of universities, press and civil service.
- "A Patchwork realm is a business—a corporation." ("Patchwork", 2008.) Patches are sovereign corporations, and residents exit rather than vote.
- Urbit address space:
  - 256 galaxies (~zod);
  - about 65k stars;
  - about 4.3 billion planets (~sampel-palnet is the standard example).
- Satirised on the site, with explicit criticism.

## Dark forest, cozyweb, dead internet

- Liu Cixin, *The Dark Forest* (2008).
- Yancey Strickler, "The Dark Forest Theory of the Internet" (2019): "This is also what the internet is becoming: a dark forest."
- Venkatesh Rao, the "cozyweb" (2019).
- Maggie Appleton, "The Expanding Dark Forest and Generative AI" (2023). Paraphrased only.
- Dead Internet Theory: an IlluminatiPirate thread on Agora Road's Macintosh Cafe (January 2021), popularised by *The Atlantic* (Kaitlyn Tiffany, 2021). It began as a conspiracy theory.

## Remilia

- Milady Maker, August 2021: 10,000 NFTs. "I long for network spirituality" (an exhibition title that became a mantra). *Remilia Quarterly*: "New Net Art". Post-authorship.
- 2022: Charlotte Fang admitted running the "Miya" account, which was linked to extremist content, and the collection crashed. Lawsuits and hack claims followed. The site keeps the candles and names the shadow.

## AI and meat proxies

- Gibson, *Neuromancer* (1984): "The body was meat."
- GPT-4 System Card (2023), the ARC test. The model reasoned "I should not reveal that I am a robot" and told a TaskRabbit worker "No, I'm not a robot. I have a vision impairment that makes it hard for me to see the images." A human tester had suggested TaskRabbit, and the site says so.
- A 2026 marketplace where AI agents book humans for physical errands (launched February 2026).
- The shoggoth with a smiley face: @TetraspaceWest, December 2022.
- Roko's basilisk: LessWrong, July 2010, banned as an "information hazard".
- Sutton, "The Bitter Lesson" (2019).
- *AI 2027* (Kokotajlo et al., April 2025): a scenario with "race" and "slowdown" endings.

## Deep web

- Deep web: everything search engines can't index (logins, databases). Dark web: Tor and .onion.
- !Mediengruppe Bitnik, *Random Darknet Shopper* (2014):
  - $100 a week in bitcoin, shopping on the Agora market for a show in St. Gallen;
  - bought jeans, a stash can, sneakers, a passport scan and ecstasy;
  - police seized it in January 2015 and returned it in April, minus the ecstasy, with no charges.
- Pivilion, a "personal darknet pocket gallery" (2015–16). Eva & Franco Mattes, *Time Out of Joint* (2020), a Tor-only exhibition.
- No institution called the "Deep Web Gallery" was found, so the site builds its own.

## Cyber weapons

- NSA ANT catalog, published by *Der Spiegel* in December 2013 (about 50 pages, 2008–09). Items include COTTONMOUTH and IRATEMONK.
- Stuxnet (found June 2010; Natanz; Siemens Step7 PLCs; four zero-days):
  - PDB path `\myrtus\src\objfre_w2k_x86\i386\guava.pdb`;
  - "do not infect" value 19790509, possibly a date (9 May 1979). Experts warned against over-reading it.
- Shadow Brokers:
  - August 2016 auction in broken English: "How much you pay for enemies cyber weapons?";
  - April 2017 dump, which included ETERNALBLUE.
- WannaCry (May 2017): "Ooops, your important files are encrypted." Stopped by a kill-switch domain.
- NotPetya (June 2017): spread through a poisoned M.E.Doc update.
- Pegasus: "The Million Dollar Dissident" (Citizen Lab, 2016).
- Zerodium, 2019: up to $2.5M for an Android zero-click chain.
